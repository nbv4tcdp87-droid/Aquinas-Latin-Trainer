= Ad primum sic proceditur.
@ I q. 1 a. 1 arg. 1
> One proceeds to the first article as follows.
! Every article opens this way. Proceditur is an impersonal passive (literally "it is proceeded"), and primum means the first article.

= Respondeo dicendum sacram doctrinam esse scientiam.
@ I q. 1 a. 2 co.
> I answer that it must be said that sacred doctrine is a science.
! Dicendum (est is understood) is a passive periphrastic: "it must be said". Here it takes an accusative and infinitive; more often Thomas writes dicendum quod with a finite verb.

= Sed contra, illud est subiectum scientiae, de quo est sermo in scientia.
@ I q. 1 a. 7 s. c.
> But on the contrary, the subject of a science is that which the science discusses.
! Literally "that is the subject of a science about which there is discourse in the science". Sed contra, "but on the contrary", introduces the authority or argument on the other side.

= Cum enim gratia non tollat naturam, sed perficiat, oportet quod naturalis ratio subserviat fidei.
@ I q. 1 a. 8 ad 2
> For since grace does not take away nature but perfects it, natural reason must serve faith.
! A famous principle: grace does not destroy nature but perfects it. Oportet quod with the subjunctive is the Summa's usual "it is necessary that".
